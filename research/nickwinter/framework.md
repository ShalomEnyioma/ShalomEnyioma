## A. Niche research and niche-finding process

### A1. His mental model

- **Niche = market + format.** For example, market "watches" plus format "hidden industry secrets" gives the niche "watch industry secrets" [3O2r @1:06].
  - The **format** is the proven part: "10 restaurants to avoid… and few that are safe", "5 olive oils to skip". The format is already validated on YouTube [3O2r @0:00–1:06].
  - The **market** is what you choose. It must be **big** in the real world and **unsaturated** on YouTube [3O2r @1:39–2:11].
- **Every viral niche goes through four stages** [3O2r @2:11–6:40]:
  1. **New format.** Someone creative invents a format and applies it to a winning market. His example is explaining finance with Family Guy-style characters. *Don't spend your time here unless you are very creative.* Formats are hard to validate with data. Let others take the risk.
  2. **Audience bend**, about **1 to 4 weeks** after the format goes viral. The same format and market are re-aimed at another audience. US channels are adapted first to UK, Canada and Australia, then to Germany, Spain, France, Brazil (Portuguese) and Russia, chosen for population size. About 90% of niches that work in the US also work elsewhere, *provided you adapt rather than translate* [3O2r @4:26].
  3. **Obvious niche bends**, roughly at the same time as audience bends, done by more advanced people. Cat psychology becomes dog psychology. "Everything Toyota" becomes "Everything Chevrolet", Audi and Ford. *These are easy to spot, so speed matters* [3O2r @4:59–5:33].
  4. **Creative niche bends**, from about **4 weeks to years** later. These need more research: cat psychology to **birds**, to **squirrels**, or to **raising kids with Japanese methods**. The last one reached 84k subs in 15 long-form videos. "The niches that actually print the most money are the ones that you come up with yourself by niche bending." Each new creative bend then starts its own cycle of audience bends [3O2r @6:06–7:46].
- **Don't copy viral channels 1:1.** "He is already making the 10K. So there's no more 10K for you." His example: copycats of a viral avatar video got 200 to 3k views [5wB9 @7:06, @10:44–11:23]. "If you have to ask me [if you're early enough], then you are not early enough" [5wB9 @11:23].
- The exception he does use himself: **porting an existing viral concept into another language.** He calls it "a viable strategy that I'm doing like a lot", for example German and Spanish versions of a US car-avatar channel [5wB9 @11:57–12:34]. But "the biggest method" for big numbers is a **niche bend nobody has done before** [5wB9 @12:34].

### A3. Step by step: from a "popping channel" to a validated niche

**Route 1: niche bending (preferred, for new niches)** [3O2r @7:46–14:23]

1. **Find a popping channel** in TubeLab, NexLev or his tracker. A good candidate has a recent video far above its own average. Examples: a channel with 130 subscribers and a 50k-view video "outperformed basically all of the other videos" [3O2r @9:57]; a sperm-whale video with 40k views in 2 days on a 30-minute video, against 1k to 45k on its other videos [3O2r @8:52–9:25].
2. **Look for the US original**, not an already audience-bent copy. About 90% of channels start in the US market [5wB9 @20:27].
3. **Screenshot the channel's video grid**, showing titles and thumbnails, and **copy the transcript of its most-viewed video** [3O2r @11:34].
4. **Run his Claude prompt** (free at `hub.nickwinter.org/nichebending`). Attach the prompt markdown, the screenshot and the transcript, and ask for **potential niche bends**. The prompt is based on his analysis of **more than 7,000 titles and more than 1,000 channels**. Claude also researches the web for market statistics [3O2r @6:40, @12:08–12:42].
5. **Validate demand with real-world market size (TAM).** Find studies or statistics: "how many people own watches or buy watches per year" (US watch revenue about $24B in 2026); "US pet supplement market $1.13B in 2024 → $2.01B by 2033" [3O2r @1:39, @12:42–13:16].
6. **Check YouTube for existing channels in the bent niche.** If none exist, "combine both, create a new niche" [3O2r @2:11]. A quick test: take the most viral title of the source channel, change the one word that sets the market or audience ("millions of **Canadian** truckers…", "millions of **UK lorry drivers**…"), and search YouTube for it. No results means an open lane. A strong channel already there means it is "over for you" [5wB9 @26:36–27:12].
7. **Sanity-check breadth.** Can you make 30 or more videos? Sunscreen alone is too narrow: "you can't just post 30 videos on sunscreen… you would burn out your audience." Make it one video on a broader channel instead [3O2r @13:50].
8. **Validate the AI's statistics yourself.** Feed back any existing channels you found, then start posting [3O2r @13:50–14:23].
9. His upcoming automated tool does the same thing: analyze the channel, research the web, estimate TAM, search YouTube for existing title patterns and outlier channels, and return only bends that haven't been done [3O2r @14:23–15:28].

**Route 2: entering or validating a niche you already like (research for ideas)** [V9js @4:16–18:22]

1. TubeLab Outliers Finder: spot a channel that recently popped.
2. Use NexLev (or TubeLab) **similar channels** to open every channel with similar branding. Ctrl+click them all [V9js @5:30–7:20].
3. For each channel, sort by **Popular** and check that it really is the same vibe (for example nostalgia, as opposed to consumer-tips content that only looks similar) [V9js @7:20–8:34].
4. **Discard channels without viral outliers.** "If the channel doesn't have viral videos, we also don't really need to look at this" [V9js @8:34].
5. **Export all videos** of each kept channel as CSV (NexLev → Export data → number of videos = total → sort Popular) [V9js @9:09–9:45].
6. Create a **Claude Project** for the niche and upload all the CSVs:
   - Ask for **"30 winning title formats"** used across many viral videos. Put them into the project **instructions** with the rule "any title must use one of these structures" [V9js @13:21–13:55].
   - Ask: "Today is [date]. Look at all the CSV files. Can you see any trends emerging in the recent videos?" [V9js @14:31–15:12].
   - Follow up: "Based on those viral ideas, come up with **10 more that haven't been done yet in this niche**." Without "haven't been done yet" it repeats ideas already in the CSVs [V9js @16:23–17:08].
7. **Comment mining** with the "Comments Exporter for YouTube" extension. He credits the tip to Wiz of YouTube. Export comments from the **most popular** videos (or a recent one with 20 to 30k views) and have AI pull video ideas from them. **Skip videos with 3 to 5k views** because there aren't enough comments to see a trend. "If the video has 100,000 views and 1,000 comments and 50% mention one thing… it's in their brains." Also do this on your own channel [V9js @10:25–12:46].
8. Ask the AI to output the ideas as **CSV**, paste them into the production sheet, then **test cheaply**. Some ideas you doubt will go viral for someone else later [V9js @15:47–17:45].

**Route 3: a scripted outlier scraper for idea flow (used on his story channel)** [KQW5 @10:39–14:29]

- An n8n workflow runs every 12 hours. It pulls the **latest 15 uploads** of a list of competitor channel IDs and flags as viral any video **older than 48 hours with at least 7,000 views**, or **12 hours old or newer with at least 4,000 views**.
- That gives him about 5 to 8 viral source videos a day, enough to post 3 a day plus a buffer.

## B. Every bending or angle technique he teaches

### B1. Niche bend (obvious)
- **Definition:** keep the format and the content style, and swap the market for an adjacent, obvious one [3O2r @4:59].
- **How:** ask what the *same audience type* also owns, does or cares about. Cats become dogs (household pets). Toyota becomes Chevrolet, Audi or Ford (other big US car brands).
- **Examples:** Filou cat psychology to dogs; Everything Toyota to Everything Chevrolet (500k views), Audi (100k) and Ford (165k) [3O2r @4:59–5:33].
- **When:** right after a format pops. You must be fast because many people spot these.

### B2. Niche bend (creative)
- **Definition:** a non-obvious jump into a new market that still fits the format's psychology [3O2r @5:33–7:46].
- **How:** brainstorm alone or with Claude and his prompt. Validate with TAM statistics and a YouTube search. Claude tends to suggest only obvious bends (pets), so the creative leap is yours [3O2r @6:40–7:11].
- **Examples:** cat psychology to **birds** (245k views 10 days after upload), to **squirrels** (one hit out of 11 videos, "not completely successful"), to **raising kids with Japanese methods** (84k subs from 15 long-form videos, up to 1.4M views) [3O2r @6:06–7:46]. Hidden industry secrets applied to **watches**; protein-powder "products to avoid" applied to **pet supplements** [3O2r @1:06, @13:16].
- **When:** any time, even years after a format emerged. This is where he says the money is.

### B3. Audience bend
- **Definition:** the same format and market, aimed at a different audience [3O2r @3:20].
- **Types:** (a) **country or language**: US → UK, Canada, Australia → DE, ES, FR, PT-BR, RU; (b) **demographic of the presenter or character**: a white presenter becomes a Black presenter, or a man becomes a woman ("Susan Parker Travels") [3O2r @3:20–4:26; 5wB9 @20:27–21:04]. A finance cartoon channel was cloned with a Black character ("Bob Invest") [mQga @0:00].
- **Rule that decides whether it works:** **only bend when the audience's reality actually differs.**
  - **Good:** truckers. UK and Australian truckers have different companies, brands, rules and problems, so a "British truckers" channel blew up. Nostalgia also works: "UK people had a different childhood than US people" [5wB9 @9:31, @23:37–24:49].
  - **Bad:** "heating sources that don't need electricity" for the UK. There is no real difference, and it is already English-speaking. The result was 200 views [5wB9 @8:54–10:09].
- **Execution:** export the source channel's videos, then ask Claude to **adapt** (never translate) every idea and script to the new audience: currency, "lorries" instead of "trucks", the persona's name [5wB9 @26:00–28:25].
- **When:** 1 to 4 weeks after a US format pops. This is the easiest path. The first mover in each audience usually wins ("he has the viral audience, he's just going to milk everything now") [5wB9 @27:12].

### B4. "Authority" re-framing (his move; the name is ours)
- **Definition:** take a proven viral concept and have a **different, more credible or more intriguing authority** present it. That creates a new audience and a fresh angle [5wB9 @10:09–10:44, @21:40–22:21].
- **Examples:** a viral "fix your home against burglars / panic room" avatar video (2.3M views) became "**12 home tricks Japanese police teach seniors living alone**". Nick says he has "this exact video with different authorities on like four channels." His own test: explaining the same topic "in the perspective of… a Norwegian". He admits that one "seems like it flopped" [5wB9 @10:09–10:44].
- **Why:** "even if you just find an authority that will only get 100,000 views, that's already monetization for you" [5wB9 @22:21].
- **Related:** the "Japanese methods" kids channel is a creative niche bend that also stacks an authority or culture frame [3O2r @7:46].

### B5. Re-making proven outliers ("parasite"; the closest thing to "proven classics")
- **Definition:** take an existing viral video, including old ones, and make your own version on your channel, re-packaged [5wB9 @34:25–35:37].
- **Nick's example:** "How to not get sick while smoking a cigar" is "just based on this video here which got 1.8 million views 5 years ago." Old brand or creator cigar videos with 350k views are "a proven video."
- **Juvon's definitions** [gL4V @16:36–19:29]:
  - **Outlier research only.** Every idea comes from title and thumbnail outliers on YouTube. No Google Trends, no "what we think will go viral", and "do not be creative" with packaging.
  - **Angles:** turn an outlier title into a template. "How people live in Bermuda" becomes "how people live in ___".
  - **Niche bending (Juvon's version):** "taking angles from another niche", meaning an outlier angle from a different niche applied to yours.
  - **Parasite:** "making the same exact video as… a old video that's extremely viral."
  - **Re-package big outlets:** news outlets post on subscriber momentum with weak packaging. Take their million-view topics, package them properly, and out-view them [gL4V @59:13–60:56].

### B6. Format-level moves (no single name)
- **"Put an avatar on it."** Take an existing working faceless concept and redo the same content with an AI avatar presenter. Examples: an airport-tips faceless niche moved to his "Magnus" avatar; the German car avatar "Andreas Rink" [5wB9 @15:34–16:47, @17:21].
- **Format transplant across markets.** His paint-explainer and finance-cartoon builds note that the same visual format works "across all different niches" [Dm9Z @0:34; mQga @0:34].
- **Sub-niching down.** He suggests a sperm-whale-only channel after seeing that the whale videos outperform a general ocean channel [3O2r @8:52–9:25]. Pet supplements rather than supplements in general [3O2r @13:16].
- **Seasonal re-skin.** His nostalgia channel pivoted to **Christmas and winter** themes when TubeLab showed those videos popping [V9js @0:36]. He notes holiday-month channels "pick up every year" [5wB9 @16:09].
- **Sub-niche or persona variants of a story format:** a revenge story "from an elderly lady's perspective, a middle-aged woman, an elderly male" [KQW5 @2:49].

### B7. Collab method (a growth technique he groups with these)
- Add a new channel as a **collaborator on a video of an established channel you run**. YouTube shows the new channel's content to the existing audience. One example went "from zero to 60,000 views a day in like 4 days" [5wB9 @1:47–2:57, @12:34]. He warns that many "secret method" screenshots are really just this.

## C. Picking and vetting niches: what he avoids, saturation signs, policy, monetization

**Saturation signs**
- Many near-identical copycats getting 200 to 3k views on the same concept [5wB9 @8:54, @14:22].
- A format that got 2M views 7 months ago in that market ("restaurants to avoid") will not repeat today [3O2r @0:34].
- A strong first mover already in your target audience, such as the British truckers channel [5wB9 @27:12].
- You are asking whether you're early enough [5wB9 @11:23].
- He is **not** against entering a crowded niche if you can out-package and out-volume weak incumbents. This is Juvon's argument, which Nick agrees with: someone posting once every 5 days with great results can be "outscaled" [gL4V @61:30].

**What he avoids**
- **Politics and fake news.** "AI avatars [don't] get you terminated. Politics get you terminated." Most new politics channels he tracks "get terminated within a week" [3gbb @22:38; I6gU @24:54].
- **Fictional content without a face** (AI stories, fictional kids content) "won't survive 2026". Even expensive, hand-edited fictional kids videos were demonetized for inauthentic content [3gbb @12:04–14:16].
- **Low-effort templated slideshows** (5 or 6 images on loop, identical thumbnails). His 2026 rule: "**If the average guy could see your channel and immediately realize it's automated, don't run it**" [I6gU @19:56]. Note that his Dec 2025 story channel was exactly this (6 looping images), which shows how fast his advice changed [KQW5 @1:06].
- **Niches too narrow for 30 or more videos** (sunscreen) [3O2r @13:50].
- **Audience bends with no real audience difference** [5wB9 @9:31].
- For ebook or avatar plays, **niches that don't solve a problem or whose viewers aren't buyers**. Tractor news is watched "as news", and few viewers buy tractors [5wB9 @14:58–15:34].

**Policy notes he gives** (see section F for our risk view)
- The "inauthentic content" policy is essentially YouTube's 2018 **repetitious content** rule: repetitive or mass-produced content, slideshows without narrative or educational value, programmatically generated content. "You don't get falsely demonetized… you breach YouTube policy in broad daylight" [I6gU @6:02–7:11, @15:31–18:15].
- **Educational content survives every wave** ("like the cockroach in an atom war"). It must be **actually** educational, with **real sources** cited in the video and the description [3gbb @15:21–18:11].
- Vary thumbnail templates so they read as a **brand style**, not a template [I6gU @19:56–20:28].
- History pattern: each demonetization wave (Reddit TTS 2019, kids' COPPA 2019, compilations 2016 to 2019) wiped out competitors. People who adapted (more transformative, more educational) then earned more [I6gU @9:57–15:31].

**Monetization considerations**
- **Older audiences (60+) mean higher RPM, longer watch time, more comments and more product buyers.** That is why his nostalgia and avatar channels target them [V9js @0:36; 5wB9 @4:10; gL4V @33:02].
- **Unit economics decide a niche's life.** His 70s and 80s nostalgia channel died when $30 to $40 freelancer videos exceeded about $30/day revenue. Automating to $5 to $10 per video made the same niche viable again, so he could plant "more seeds" [V9js @1:14–2:27; Dm9Z @2:14–3:20].
- **Language ports can have terrible RPM.** One German channel monetized in under two weeks but made only $109 on 123k views in 28 days, so he scrapped it [V9js @3:03–3:39].
- **Backend products** (ebooks, physical books, newsletters) can roughly double AdSense and pay before monetization. On a scrapped channel whose best video had 6k views he still made about $300 in ebooks [5wB9 @36:48–38:38; gL4V @36:22–38:03].
- **Q4 RPMs are higher**, so launch before Q4 [5wB9 @0:35; gL4V @29:04].

## D. Packaging, scripting, production, cadence and growing to monetization

### D1. Packaging (titles and thumbnails)
- **Titles come from proven title formats.** Claude extracts about 30 winning structures from competitor CSVs, and every new title must use one of them. "The title format just gets milked as long as it works." His own top German videos reuse one structure [V9js @13:21–14:31].
- **Packaging is the money task.** Juvon says titles and thumbnails are "the number one task that controls the money" and should be delegated last. Do strictly outlier-based, non-creative packaging [gL4V @23:25–24:32, @19:29].
- **Diagnosis rule:** "even after 10 videos, if you get only 1,000 views a day or… 500 views a day, then it's your packaging that's a problem and not the channel trust" [5wB9 @30:11].
- **Study CTR and AVD on every video** and try to improve both each time. "On your flops… you're going to learn more" [gL4V @28:31].
- **Thumbnails:** a consistent brand style but **not an identical template** [I6gU @19:56–20:28]. His automated thumbnail tool and workflows adapt the source thumbnail's text and layout. On the story channel he made thumbnails "basically 90% similar" to the source, which is a red flag (see F) [KQW5 @3:54–4:29, @31:46–33:58].
- **Seasonal packaging** (Christmas, winter) when the niche data shows it [V9js @0:36].

### D2. Scripting and retention
- **Base scripts on a proven reference.** "Take an existing viral script, take the original American channel, put it into Claude and ask him to **adapt** it" to the new audience or persona. Never say "translate" or "convert". "Adapt" makes the model change currency, terminology and names [5wB9 @27:12–28:25].
- **Build a general system prompt from competitor transcripts.** Paste a top video's transcript into Claude and ask for a *general* system prompt for videos like this, with the target length, outputting only the prompt. Iterate on what you don't like [mQga @15:30–16:05].
- **Structure first, then script.** Have one step generate the video structure from the title and a second step write the script. Models focus on one thing at a time [mQga @14:55].
- **Three-act structure** for scripts [mQga @16:05–16:40].
- **Length control by character count** (for example about 1,250 characters per chapter). Models now hit a target length reliably [mQga @17:14; Dm9Z @30:28].
- **Long scripts get chunked** to avoid "context rot" (repetition, drift) on very long outputs. 10 to 20 minute scripts can be generated in one or two calls [mQga @13:08–14:17].
- **Write the prompt itself in the target language** for non-English channels. The model draws better on that language's style [KQW5 @29:29].
- **Model choice:** Claude Opus on high or x-high reasoning for scripts and for building tools. "If you're writing scripts with GPT, it's already over for you." Use Claude sub-agents to adapt 5 reference transcripts in parallel [5wB9 @28:25–29:36, @33:48].
- **Real sources:** a web-research step gathers real facts, shows them as infographics, and lists source links in the description [3gbb @17:05–18:11].
- **Retention through immersion (avatar channels):** AI-generated images that show *exactly* what the narrator describes, plus an avatar speaking directly to the viewer. Example opener: "Morning and your head hurts. Last night you smoked a good cigar on the porch…". He says AVD and sub conversion are much higher than regular faceless [5wB9 @17:59–19:13; gL4V @36:54–37:28].
- **Auto-ideation from your own channel:** daily analysis of new videos' performance and comments to spot breakouts and write the next script [5wB9 @32:32].

### D3. Production workflow and tools
- **Automation is "not optional anymore",** at least partly. The aim is to replace freelancers and editors [3gbb @0:00–1:40].
- **His stack (n8n builds):** self-hosted **n8n** on a **Hetzner** server (about $20/month); **MinIO** for S3-style storage; **NocoDB** as the idea and status sheet (Google Sheets and Drive rate-limit at scale); **NCA Toolkit** as the render API (FFmpeg); **GenAI Pro** for voice ("like ElevenLabs, just way cheaper", 4 concurrent jobs); **OpenRouter** for models (including a free vision model for reading thumbnail text); custom APIs for thumbnails, infographics, map animations, image search and checking, and a yt-dlp downloader [YMc9 @0:00–2:44; mQga @2:23–6:32, @20:15; KQW5 @6:44–10:05, @32:51; Dm9Z @7:09–8:15, @38:49; 3gbb @5:34–7:43].
- **Workflow pattern:** an organizer workflow with a single settings node. It picks the next "queued" idea from the sheet, slugifies the title into a project path, generates the script (structure, then three-act script), splits the script into about 1,500-character chunks, has parallel "workers" make voice and images per chunk (video clips for chunk 1), renders the chunks, concatenates them, generates the thumbnail, and marks the row "done". A limit node prevents n8n item explosions [mQga @2:23–23:15; Dm9Z].
- **Costs he quotes:**
  - Story channel: under $0.30 per video (6 looping images and a voice) [KQW5 @1:41].
  - Nostalgia: $5 to 10 per video, against $30 to 40 with freelancers [V9js @1:51].
  - Avatar: $3 to 5 per 20-minute video [5wB9 @33:48].
  - His "Vitruvian-killer" system: about $0.15/min ($3 for 20 minutes, $9 for an hour) [wsK4 @17:19].
  - VidRush (he calls it "Vitruvian" in places): about $2 to 2.70/min, roughly $35 to 40 per 20-minute video [3gbb @2:47–3:22; 5wB9 @19:50].
  - Headcast by Algro: about $0.70/min [5wB9 @31:21].
  - Juvon: about $2.20 per video at 80 a day, but some channels still cost $40 a video and are profitable [gL4V @46:20–47:26].
- **Avatars:** HeyGen (he wrote a browser-automation bot to get around HeyGen's API minute limits; see F). His own app creates the persona, the HeyGen avatar, ebooks and a sales page "with one click" [3gbb @6:06–6:38; 5wB9 @32:32, @37:26].

### D4. Upload cadence
- **Prepare 5 to 10 videos before launch and schedule 5 to 10 days ahead** with YouTube's scheduler, so you can run many channels without daily panic [5wB9 @29:36–30:11].
- **Daily or twice-daily posting is fine** when videos are cheap, because it lets you "test around" ideas [V9js @17:45]. His story channel ran **3 a day** [KQW5 @13:55].
- **Extreme volume works in some cases:** Juvon posted 75 to 80 videos a day on one politics avatar channel with "no issues" [gL4V @7:47–8:53]. Nick himself didn't post daily at the start of his cigar channel: "just gave the channel some time to kind of pick up" [5wB9 @1:11].

### D5. Growing a new channel to monetization fast
- **Patience window:** most of his channels "pick up only after two weeks", and many never do. "Give it 5 to 10 videos minimum" before judging [5wB9 @2:57, @30:11].
- **Fast cases:** his nostalgia port monetized "in under two weeks" (6 Nov to around 20 Nov) [V9js @3:03]. Tim Danilov's students got monetized after 4 to 5 videos in zero-competition bends (other creator, `nKtm3knn8aA @4:53`).
- **Watch hours vs subs:** on regular faceless channels he usually hits 4,000 watch hours **before** 1,000 subs, sometimes with only about 200 subs. Avatar and persona channels hit subs first [gL4V @37:28].
- **Launch levers:** (1) a niche bend nobody has done yet; (2) the collab method with an existing channel; (3) a "high trust" channel (his aged-account warm-up test; see F); (4) packaging that copies proven title structures; (5) volume and cheap production so you can keep testing.
- **When to kill a channel:** when cost per video exceeds daily revenue and it stays there. He stopped the nostalgia channel at $10/day, and the avatar channel at 300 to 500 views a day [V9js @1:51; 5wB9 @38:38].

## E. One-page checklist: run this every time we look for a niche

**1. Find a seed (20 to 30 min)**
- [ ] Go to TubeLab → Outliers Finder → Discover ("new viral videos from small channels") and the Niche Finder, filtered to our languages, sorted by views per day. Optionally use the NexLev niche finder.
- [ ] Shortlist channels where a **recent video is far above that channel's own average**, especially young or small channels.
- [ ] Trace each one back to the **US original** if it is already a port.

**2. Break the seed into market + format**
- [ ] Market: who watches, and what real-world category is it?
- [ ] Format: title structure, video style, narrator or persona, thumbnail style.
- [ ] Which stage is it in: new format, audience-bent, obvious bends, or creative bends?

**3. Generate bends (Claude, with his niche-bending prompt, a channel screenshot and the top transcript)**
- [ ] **Audience bends:** UK, CA, AU, then DE, ES, FR, PT-BR. Different presenter demographic. *Only if the audience's reality actually differs.*
- [ ] **Obvious niche bends:** adjacent markets with the same audience type.
- [ ] **Creative niche bends:** non-obvious markets that fit the format's appeal.
- [ ] **Authority re-frames:** "what [credible group or culture] teaches about ___".
- [ ] **Re-made proven outliers:** old high-view videos in the topic, from any channel or brand.

**4. Validate each bend**
- [ ] **TAM:** a real market-size or participation statistic (revenue, number of owners or enthusiasts). Check the AI's figures.
- [ ] **YouTube search:** swap the market or audience word into the source's best title and search. Is the lane empty, weak, or held by a strong first mover?
- [ ] **Breadth:** can we list 30 or more distinct video ideas?
- [ ] **Demographic and RPM:** older or affluent audience? Ad-friendly? Is there a product or backend fit?
- [ ] **Policy:** not politics or fake news; not fiction; can it be **genuinely educational and sourced**; does it avoid looking templated?
- [ ] **Unit economics:** at our cost per video, how many views per video break even?

**5. Build the idea bank (for the chosen niche)**
- [ ] Similar-channels search → open all → keep only same-vibe channels **with outliers**.
- [ ] Export every channel's videos to CSV → Claude Project → **30 winning title formats** → project instructions.
- [ ] Ask for "trends emerging in recent videos", then "10 more that haven't been done yet in this niche".
- [ ] Export comments from the top 2 or 3 videos (100k+ views) → mine for ideas.
- [ ] Load 20 or more ideas into the production sheet.

**6. Launch**
- [ ] Have 5 to 10 videos produced and scheduled before day 1.
- [ ] Judge only after 5 to 10 videos: low views with OK impressions means packaging; zero impressions means a channel or launch problem.
- [ ] Track CTR and AVD on every upload, and re-mine our own comments weekly.

## F. Contradictions to common practice, and policy risks for us

### F1. Where he contradicts common advice (claims, not proven facts)
- **Channel warm-up is pointless.** Watching niche videos, commenting, YouTube Premium or a Pixel phone don't raise trust. "It's mainly the Gmail that determines the trust." **Account age doesn't matter** (any account from before 2025 is fine). **Proxies matter less than X claims** [wsK4 @0:00–7:10].
- **Trust only matters at launch.** After 10 videos, low views mean bad packaging, not low trust [5wB9 @30:11].
- **Don't copy winning channels**, even though copying is the common beginner advice. Yet he ports channels 1:1 into other languages and made a source thumbnail "90% similar" himself [5wB9 @7:06; KQW5 @3:54].
- **Ideas:** Nick prefers coming up with ideas himself, with AI help, over fully automated idea generation [V9js @15:47]. His guest Juvon says the opposite: "do not be creative", only outlier research. Both reject Google Trends [gL4V @19:29, @60:19].
- **Posting caps are a myth:** 75 to 80 videos a day on one channel with no strikes (Juvon) [gL4V @8:20–8:53].
- **Subscriber counts and play buttons are meaningless:** a 4.3k-sub channel made over $6k a month [KQW5 @0:33].
- **AI voices and AI avatars aren't penalised;** politics is what gets terminated. "Correlation causation fallacy" [3gbb @20:24–23:11].
- **"Falsely demonetized" is almost always a real policy breach.** The rules have been the same since 2018 [I6gU @17:09–18:15].
- **Don't bother if you have to ask whether you're early** [5wB9 @11:23].

### F2. Policy and ethics risks. We should NOT copy these.
> ⚠️ **High risk.** These practices appear in his videos. Several break Google/YouTube Terms of Service or the spam, deceptive-practices and circumvention policies, or create consumer-protection exposure. Any one of them could get a client channel or AdSense account terminated. Some are plainly deceptive.

1. **Buying aged Gmail/YouTube accounts** (from a marketplace he links) and running them through **anti-detect browsers (AdsPower)** and **residential proxies** to hide who operates them. He himself traces this toolkit to blackhat forums [wsK4 @7:44–14:16; I6gU @2:43–3:16]. Buying and selling accounts and masking identity to run many channels breaks Google's terms. It also risks **circumvention** enforcement that links and terminates related channels. **We use only our own legitimately created client accounts.**
2. **Fake human personas selling products:** "my AI generated cigar expert. Doesn't exist, doesn't smoke", selling ebooks to a 60+ audience who "don't see it as an AI avatar channel. They see it as a real personal brand" [5wB9 @0:00, @4:10]. This is deceptive toward elderly buyers. It conflicts with YouTube's **altered or synthetic content disclosure** for realistic AI people and creates consumer-protection exposure (fake endorsements or expertise). **Not for us.** If we ever use an avatar, it must be disclosed and must not claim a real person's life, credentials or experience.
3. **Evading AI detection:** use non-Google image models so Gemini can't detect **SynthID** and "Google cannot see that this is an AI avatar" [3gbb @21:30–22:38; I6gU @23:46]. This is deliberate evasion of provenance and disclosure systems. **Avoid.** Tick the AI disclosure honestly where the policy requires it.
4. **Copying or re-uploading others' content:** making a source video's thumbnail "90% similar", scraping viral US stories and re-voicing them in other languages, and "take an existing viral script… adapt it" [KQW5 @3:54–4:29, @5:36–6:10; 5wB9 @27:12]. This risks **reused or inauthentic content**, **copyright** claims (scripts are copyrighted works), and makes our content generic. **We use other videos as inspiration for topics and structure only, and write original, researched scripts.**
5. **Mass-produced, templated output:** 6 looping images, identical thumbnails, AI fiction, and 80 videos a day of politics "news" (Juvon). Nick himself now says this gets hit by inauthentic-content enforcement [I6gU @19:56; 3gbb @12:04]. For us that means fewer, better, sourced educational videos with real visual variety.
6. **Bypassing third-party limits:** a browser bot "pretending to be a human" to get around HeyGen's API limits [3gbb @6:06–6:38], and a yt-dlp downloader behind proxies so "YouTube doesn't connect our download requests" [3gbb @9:53–10:26]. Both break those platforms' terms.
7. **Politics and fake-news niches** at high volume (Juvon's channel). Nick himself warns these get terminated [3gbb @22:38].
8. **Income claims** in the videos (for example $216k in 30 days, $12k channel) come from screenshots we can't verify. Several of his "leaked" channels were later privated or scrapped [V9js @18:22]. Treat these as marketing for his Skool community and tools.

### F3. What is safe and useful for us
- The **market + format** model, the **audience, niche and authority** bends, **TAM validation**, **title-format mining**, **comment mining**, the **"adapt, don't translate"** principle (for our *own* scripts), **structure-then-script**, the **three-act** structure, **sourced educational** content, **batch-and-schedule**, the **5 to 10 video patience window**, and **CTR/AVD discipline**.
